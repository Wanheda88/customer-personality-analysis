# Customer Personality Analysis

## Project Overview
This project explores customer demographics and purchasing behavior using Microsoft Excel. The goal is to identify customer spending patterns, compare different age groups, and understand which product categories contribute most to overall customer spending.

## Dataset
- **Source:** Kaggle – Customer Personality Analysis
- **Records:** 2,240 customers
- **Original columns:** 29
- **Tools:** Microsoft Excel

## Project Objectives
- Explore customer demographics and purchasing behavior.
- Analyze spending patterns across different age groups.
- Identify the highest-performing product categories.
- Calculate each product category's contribution to total spending.
- Develop business recommendations based on the findings.

## Data Preparation
- Reviewed missing values and potential outliers.
- Checked for duplicate records.
- Created an Age column.
- Identified unusual age values.
- Grouped customers into age categories.
- Created a Total Spending column.
- Used IF and SUM formulas for data transformation.

## Analysis & Excel Techniques
- Excel formulas (IF, SUM).
- PivotTables.
- Data aggregation.
- Customer segmentation.
- Percentage calculations.
- Column chart visualization.

## Key Findings

### Customer Spending by Age Group
- Middle-aged customers contributed approximately 58.65% of total spending.
- Older customers had the highest average spending per customer (722.69).
- Young customers contributed approximately 11.01% of total spending.

### Product Category Performance
- Wines accounted for approximately 50.17% of total spending.
- Meat accounted for approximately 27.56%.
- Together, wines and meat represented approximately 77.73% of total spending.
- Fruits had the lowest spending contribution at approximately 4.34%.

## Business Recommendations
- Prioritize marketing strategies for wines and meat products.
- Explore opportunities to increase engagement among younger customers.
- Investigate purchasing behavior among older customers.
- Use customer segmentation for targeted marketing strategies.

## Data Limitations
- 24 records contain missing income values.
- Three records contain unusual age values.
- One unusually high income value (666,666) requires further investigation.
- Some marital-status categories contain inconsistent or unusual values.

## Conclusion
This project provided practical experience in data preparation, customer segmentation, PivotTable analysis, Excel formulas, and business insight generation.

It demonstrates how Microsoft Excel can transform raw customer data into meaningful information that supports business decisions.
